Idea 1 of 4

Waves

The primes look random. Riemann found that their count is a smooth curve plus a sum of waves, one wave for each zero of the zeta function. The Riemann Hypothesis says every one of those waves is as quiet as a wave can be.

The staircase and the waves

Climb one step at every prime power pᵏ, a step of height log p. That gives the staircase ψ(x) in gold. The smooth line is x, the trend. Each zero ρ = ½ + iγ adds a wave −2 Re(x^ρ / ρ). Add the waves and the curve snaps onto the staircase.

0

Exact formula (von Mangoldt, 1895): ψ(x) = x − Σ_ρ x^ρ/ρ − log 2π − ½ log(1 − x⁻²). Zero heights are from Odlyzko’s tables. The sum uses the first N zeros and their mirror images.

One wave

A zero’s height γ sets how fast its wave wiggles. Its left–right position β sets how fast it grows, like x^β. On the line, β = ½, so every wave grows like √x and no faster. Move a zero off the line and watch its wave outgrow everything.

1 0.50

Drawn: 2 x^β cos(γ log x) against the band ±2√x. This is a sketch of the growth only; the factor 1/|ρ| that sets each wave’s size is left out. Zeros come in mirror pairs, so a zero left of the line has a twin on the right.
